# Interview Script — read-aloud version

*Companion to `interview-guide.md` (full rationale there). This is just the questions, in order, ready to run. Print or keep open on a second screen. Target: 30-40 min.*

**Before the call:** open their survey response (`research/survey/responses_raw.csv`) and note: library size, utility-photo score (Q3), what they said their last hard-to-find photo was, and whether they searched or scrolled. Don't re-ask these — use them to pick Task A/C follow-ups.

---

### Opening (1 min) — say this
> "Thanks for doing this. I'm researching how people find old photos on their phone — there are no wrong answers, and this isn't a test of you, it's a test of the app. I'll ask you to actually find a couple of photos while you talk through what you're doing — please narrate as you go, even the boring parts. Okay to record your screen for my notes?"

---

### 1. Warm-up (4 min)
1. "How long have you been using [Google Photos / their app]? Roughly how many photos would you guess are in there?"
2. "Besides actual photos, what else ends up in there — screenshots, receipts, documents, things people send you?"
3. "Do you use more than one Google account, or has your library ever moved between phones or accounts?"
   - *If yes →* "Has that ever made it harder to find something — not knowing which account it's in?"
4. "When's the last time you went looking for an old photo and it was a hassle? What was it, roughly?"

---

### 2. Tasks (20 min) — **do not help, do not suggest search or scrolling**

**Task A**
> "Think of a specific photo you know you have, from at least a year ago, that you think you'd struggle to find right now. Don't tell me where it is yet — just describe it to me first."

Ask before they touch the phone:
- "What do you remember about it?"
- "What don't you remember, or aren't sure about?"

> "Okay — go find it."

*Watch silently. Note their first move (search bar? scrolling? an album?) before you say anything.*

**Task B**
> "Now find a photo you took of a document, receipt, label, or something on a screen — something you photographed to remember information, not for the memory itself."

**Task C**
> "Last one — think of a photo where you genuinely have no idea what year, or even season, it was taken. You just know it exists. Find that one."
*(If they can't think of one: "How about something from years ago — a childhood photo, something like that?")*

For each task, jot down (use the table in `interview-guide.md` if typing):
- What they remembered / forgot
- First action, unprompted
- Exact query typed, if any
- What happened, second attempt
- Found it? How long? Where exactly did it get hard?

---

### 3. Reflection (8 min)
5. "You just [searched / scrolled] just now — is that what you'd normally do, or did today feel different?"
6. "When it didn't work right away, what did you wish you could've told the app that you couldn't?"
7. "If you had to describe that photo to a friend who had access to your library, what would you say to them?"
8. "What do you usually do when this happens — keep trying, ask someone else, give up? Do lost photos usually turn up eventually, or do some just stay lost?"
9. "How often would you say this happens — daily, weekly, monthly, rarely? And when it does, does it usually matter, or is it just a bit annoying?"
10. "Have you ever given up on a photo you knew you had, and never went back to it? What was it — and did it matter?"

---

### 4. Close (2 min)
11. "Would you be up for a quick 15-minute follow-up in a couple weeks, where I'd show you something I'm building and get your reaction?"
> "That's everything — thank you, genuinely useful."

---

## Right after the call (do this before the next one — 5 min)
Fill `research/interviews/P<n>.md`:
- Profile + segment hints
- Per-task table
- Top 3 verbatim quotes
- Did unprompted behavior match their survey answer?
- Did the date-blind task (C) actually break retrieval?
- Any fragmentation mention?
- One surprise
