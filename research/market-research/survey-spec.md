# Memory & Photo Retrieval Survey — Google Forms Spec

**Purpose:** get what public reviews can't give us — frequency, stakes, and segment validation — using the same memory-cue vocabulary as the discovery engine, so the two datasets triangulate. Also doubles as an interview-recruiting funnel (last question).

**Target:** ~40-60 responses is enough to see patterns; don't over-invest in reach. Share with people who use Google Photos (or Apple Photos/any photo app) as their main library — not just tech-savvy friends, try to get a mix of ages/usage habits.

**Est. completion time:** 4-5 minutes. Keep it that short — every extra question loses respondents.

---

## How to build it (3 min)
1. Go to **forms.google.com** → blank form.
2. Title: **"How do you find old photos?"** Description: *"A short (4-min) research survey for a product design project — not affiliated with Google. Answers are anonymous unless you opt in at the end."*
3. Under Settings (⚙️) → turn ON "Collect email addresses" = **OFF** (keep anonymous) and turn on "Response validation" only where noted below.
4. Add each question below in order, matching the type shown. Section breaks = click the "Add section" icon (≡ with lines) between groups.

---

## Section 1 — About your library (screener / segmentation)

**Q1.** How long have you been using a photo app like Google Photos or Apple Photos?
*Type: Multiple choice*
- Less than 1 year
- 1–3 years
- 3–5 years
- More than 5 years

**Q2.** Roughly how many photos/videos are in your library?
*Type: Multiple choice*
- Under 500
- 500 – 2,000
- 2,000 – 10,000
- More than 10,000
- Not sure

**Q3.** Besides personal photos, how often do you save things like screenshots, receipts, documents, medicine labels, or whiteboards to your photo library?
*Type: Linear scale, 1–5*
- 1 = Never · 5 = All the time

---

## Section 2 — A photo you couldn't find

**Q4.** Think of the last time you looked for an OLD photo (more than 6 months old) and struggled to find it. In one sentence, what was it?
*Type: Paragraph (short answer)* — optional, but encourage it: *"e.g. 'a photo of a prescription label from when I was sick', 'a picture from a friend's birthday two years ago'"*

**Q5.** Before you started searching, what did you remember about it? *(select all that apply)*
*Type: Checkboxes*
- Roughly when it was (e.g. "last winter", "around when I moved")
- The exact date
- Where it was taken
- Who was in it
- What it looked like (colors, objects, setting)
- Words or text that were in the photo
- How you got it (e.g. someone sent it, a screenshot, downloaded)
- Why you took it / what it meant
- None of these — I just knew it existed

**Q6.** What had you forgotten or were unsure about? *(select all that apply — same list)*
*Type: Checkboxes* (repeat the same 9 options as Q5)

**Q7.** How did you try to find it? *(select all that apply)*
*Type: Checkboxes*
- Typed a search term
- Scrolled manually by date
- Looked in a specific album
- Searched for the person's face
- Searched by place/map
- Asked someone else if they had it
- Gave up without trying much
- Other: ___

**Q8.** Did you find it?
*Type: Multiple choice*
- Yes, quickly
- Yes, but it took several tries
- No, I gave up
- No, I'm still looking / haven't tried again

**Q9.** [Show only if Q8 = "Yes, but it took several tries" or "No"] What went wrong, or what eventually worked?
*Type: Paragraph* — optional

---

## Section 3 — Frequency & stakes

**Q10.** How often does this happen — you know a photo/video exists but you struggle to find it?
*Type: Multiple choice*
- Weekly or more
- A few times a month
- A few times a year
- Rarely
- This was the first time

**Q11.** When it happens, how much does it usually matter?
*Type: Multiple choice*
- Just a minor annoyance
- Genuinely frustrating in the moment
- Sometimes really matters (e.g. I needed it for proof, a memory, a task)
- Varies a lot

**Q12.** Have you ever given up looking for a photo you were sure you had?
*Type: Multiple choice*
- Yes, and I still think about it sometimes
- Yes, but I forgot about it afterward
- No, I usually find it eventually
- Not sure / never tried hard enough to say

## Section 4 — Wrap-up + recruiting

**Q13.** Which photo app do you mainly use?
*Type: Multiple choice*
- Google Photos
- Apple Photos / iCloud
- Samsung Gallery
- Other: ___

**Q14.** Would you be open to a 30-minute video call where I ask you to find a couple of old photos while you think out loud? (No prep needed, totally informal.)
*Type: Multiple choice*
- Yes — please contact me
- Maybe, tell me more first
- No thanks

**Q15.** [Show only if Q14 ≠ "No thanks"] Best way to reach you (email or phone):
*Type: Short answer* — optional

## After you publish

1. Click **Send** → copy the link → share it (WhatsApp/email/social). Aim for 40-60 responses over ~3-4 days — don't let this block interviews, run them in parallel.
2. Responses auto-collect into a linked Google Sheet (Responses tab → green Sheets icon) and Google Forms gives free auto-generated charts per question (Responses → Summary) — good enough for your deck, no extra tool needed.
3. When you have responses, export the Sheet (File → Download → CSV) and send it back to me — I'll cross-tab it against the discovery-engine findings (same cue categories) and fold both into the Part 2 slide.
